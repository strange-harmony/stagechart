STAGE CHART for Mac
===================

Turns a chord chart PDF (MultiTracks, SongSelect, or one downloaded from Planning Center)
into a stage chart: light background, two columns, 17pt lyrics, same key as the original.
The stage chart is saved in the same folder as the original. The original is never changed.

Works on any Mac from macOS 11 (Big Sur) onward, Apple silicon or Intel. Nothing else to install.


INSTALL (one time, about 2 minutes)
-----------------------------------
1. Double-click "Stage Chart.zip" to unzip it (if your Mac hasn't already).
2. Drag "Stage Chart" into your Applications folder.
3. Double-click "Stage Chart". Your Mac will say it can't check the app for malicious
   software, because it wasn't bought from the App Store. Click "Done" (or "OK").
4. Open System Settings > Privacy & Security. Scroll down to the message
   "Stage Chart was blocked..." and click "Open Anyway". Enter your Mac password
   if asked, then click "Open".
5. That's it. From now on it opens with a normal double-click.

If step 4 doesn't show "Open Anyway", open the Terminal app, paste this line, and press Return:

    xattr -dr com.apple.quarantine "/Applications/Stage Chart.app"

Then double-click Stage Chart again.


USING IT
--------
* Open Stage Chart and click "Choose PDFs...". You can pick several charts at once.
* Or drag chart PDFs onto the Stage Chart icon in the Dock or in Applications.
* Each stage chart is saved next to its original, named like
      "God of the Promise - Stage Chart - D (Light).pdf"
  Click "Show in Finder" or "Open Chart" to see it.
* The first time it saves into Downloads, Desktop or Documents, your Mac asks whether
  Stage Chart may access that folder. Click "Allow".

What it does to each chart:
* Light colors: black lyrics, dark red chords, blue cues.
* Two columns, 17pt lyrics. If the chart runs longer than the original, it tightens the
  spacing to try to match the original's page count (the text stays 17pt).
* Same key as the original. It never transposes.
* Back-to-back repeated sections are shown once with "x2" (with each pass's cue).
* Bridges and other sections that come back later are printed again, in order, with their cues.
* Slash chords are written properly, for example D/F# (some PDFs drop the sharp).

Worth knowing:
* Always give a new chart a quick look before rehearsal. Chord placement is read from the
  PDF's layout, so an unusual chart can put a chord a syllable early or late.
* Scanned charts (a picture of a page, with no real text) can't be read.
* Handwritten notes aren't added. Those still need to be added by hand.
* If something goes wrong, details are written to ~/Library/Logs/Stage Chart.log
